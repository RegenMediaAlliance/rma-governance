# Briefing: Legal Personhood for Rivers, the Magpie and the United States

*Research briefing, October 14, 2026. Sources are linked at the end. Status of each case is as reported in public sources; check current status before relying on it.*

## Bottom line

- **Canada:** The Magpie River in Quebec (Mutehekau Shipu) holds legal personhood recognized jointly by an Innu nation and a non-Indigenous regional municipality. That goes further than any US case. It is not recognized by the Quebec provincial government or by Canada.
- **United States:** River personhood exists only under tribal law. The Klamath River (Yurok Tribe, 2019) is the clearest case. No state or federal law grants it. City and county rights-of-nature measures have been struck down by courts or overridden by state law.

## The Magpie River (Mutehekau Shipu), Quebec

**Who and when.** Two parallel resolutions: the Innu Council of Ekuanitshit (January 2021) and the Minganie Regional County Municipality (February 2021), a regular Quebec municipal government. The resolutions were drafted with the International Observatory on the Rights of Nature and the Mutehekau Shipu Alliance (the Innu Council, the Minganie RCM, SNAP Quebec and the Observatory).

**What it grants.** Legal personhood and nine rights:

1. The right to flow.
2. The right to respect for its natural cycles.
3. The right for its natural evolution to be protected and preserved.
4. The right to maintain its natural biodiversity.
5. The right to fulfill its essential functions within its ecosystem.
6. The right to maintain its integrity.
7. The right to be safe from pollution.
8. The right to regenerate and be restored.
9. The right to sue.

**Who speaks for it.** Legal guardians act on the river's behalf; reporting names the members of the Innu Council of Ekuanitshit as guardians.

**Limits.** The province has not declared the river a protected area, and no provincial or federal law backs the resolutions. Hydro-Quebec, whose dam plans were part of the backdrop, is not bound by a municipal resolution. We found no report of the resolutions being tested in court. Their strength so far is political: joint Indigenous and settler-government recognition.

## United States

| Case | Who | Year | Type | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Klamath River (CA/OR) | Yurok Tribal Council | May 2019 | Rights of the river; a person for purposes of tribal court | Holds in Yurok tribal court; not tested in state or federal court |
| Snake River (ID/WA/OR) | Nez Perce Tribe General Council | June 2020 | Living entity with rights, represented by guardians | Passed the General Council and sent to the Executive Committee; final adoption not confirmed in public sources |
| Manoomin (wild rice), MN | White Earth Band of Ojibwe | 2018 | Inherent rights | A 2021 tribal-court case brought in the rice's name was later dismissed |
| Ho-Chunk Nation, WI | Tribal constitution | 2018 | Rights of nature | Tribal |
| Ponca Nation, OK | Tribal statute | 2017 | Rights of nature | Tribal |
| Lake Erie | City of Toledo, by voter initiative | 2019 | Rights, not personhood | Struck down by federal court, 2020 |
| Wekiva and Econlockhatchee rivers | Orange County, FL | 2020 | Right to clean water | Overridden by Florida law; case dismissed |
| Tamaqua, Pittsburgh, Grant Township, PA | Municipal ordinances | 2006 to 2014 | Rights of nature | Mostly struck down or in litigation |
| Lincoln County, OR | County ordinance | 2017 | Rights of nature | Struck down |

**Klamath details.** The Yurok resolution establishes the river's rights to exist, flourish and naturally evolve, along with a clean environment and a stable climate, and authorizes cases in tribal court on the river's behalf as a person. It followed droughts and dam operations that produced dangerously low flows and high water temperatures.

## Rights of nature versus legal personhood

- **Rights of nature:** nature holds rights, such as to exist, flow or regenerate.
- **Legal personhood:** nature is itself a legal person that can sue and be represented, usually by named guardians.
- The Magpie and the Klamath are personhood cases. Most US municipal measures granted rights only.

## Why it matters for regenerative media and governance

- **Tribal sovereignty is the durable route in the US.** Municipal routes are usually preempted by state law.
- **The Magpie model is the step the US has not taken:** an Indigenous nation and a local settler government recognizing the same river together, with named guardians.
- **Organizations to follow:** Earth Law Center, the International Observatory on the Rights of Nature, the Community Environmental Legal Defense Fund (CELDF), the Center for Democratic and Environmental Rights, and the Eco Jurisprudence Monitor.
